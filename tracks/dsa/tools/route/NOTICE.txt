route's maps (tracks/dsa/tools/route): where they come from, and on what terms
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Data       london.map: the roads of central London, 17260 junctions and
           22498 roads, as plain text (lib/roadgraph.h has the format).
           Map data (c) OpenStreetMap contributors,
           https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright
Source     OpenStreetMap, https://www.openstreetmap.org, fetched once on
           2026-11-14 from the Overpass API
           (https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter) with the query that is
           QUERY in gen.py: every way tagged highway=trunk, primary,
           secondary, tertiary (and their _link roads), unclassified,
           residential, living_street or pedestrian, not area=yes, not
           access=private or no, in the box below, with its nodes. The
           extract says its data is OpenStreetMap's as of
           2026-11-14T13:48:38Z (timestamp_osm_base). It was 12128591
           bytes of JSON, SHA-256 bb0437114eb4a342cd5c2708195d2829
           90e278002ab1def842251d243ce0eb06; it is not shipped (gen.py
           makes the map from it, and anyone can fetch it again).
Bounds     South 51.475, west -0.210, north 51.545, east -0.030 (degrees,
           WGS84): central London, on both banks of the Thames; 12457 by
           7783 metres on the map.
Changed    The map is made from the extract by gen.py ("python3
           tools/route/gen.py osm EXTRACT.json"), whose opening comment
           gives every step: keep the nodes in the box, cutting ways where
           they leave it; put them on a flat map in whole metres from the
           box's south-west corner; keep the junctions (the nodes where
           other than two pieces of road meet) and drop the bends, each
           road between two junctions one line with its length, the sum of
           its pieces rounded up to a metre; drop loops onto one junction,
           and of two roads between the same junctions keep the shorter;
           number the junctions south to north. Nothing else of
           OpenStreetMap is kept: no names, tags, ids or one-way streets.
           486185 bytes, SHA-256 8103ec114d66d0d6089836b7a393a160
           eeb158e38174b33cac0a04c3c8d07af6. gen.py makes the same bytes
           from the same extract.
Licence    OpenStreetMap is open data under the Open Data Commons Open
           Database License 1.0 (ODbL),
           https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/. london.map is a
           Derivative Database of it (ODbL 4.4 b: a substantial part of
           its contents in a new database), so london.map is under the
           ODbL too, and not under this repository's MIT licence. What the
           licence and the OpenStreetMap Foundation's attribution
           guidelines
           (https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Attribution_Guidelines)
           ask, and where it is done:
           - Attribution to OpenStreetMap, and that the data is under the
             ODbL, with the URL openstreetmap.org/copyright: in this file,
             in the comment at the top of london.map, and on the site's
             credits for the track.
           - The licence's URI with the database (ODbL 4.2): at the top of
             london.map and here.
           - Share-alike (ODbL 4.4) and access (4.6): london.map, the whole
             Derivative Database, is offered here under the ODbL, free of
             charge, with the method that made it (gen.py).
           - Pictures made from it are Produced Works (ODbL 4.3): every SVG
             route draws from london.map carries, in its bottom right
             corner, the map's credit line, "Map data from OpenStreetMap
             (openstreetmap.org/copyright), under the Open Database License
             (ODbL 1.0)". A lesson figure made from such a picture keeps
             that line, or says it beside the figure.
           OpenStreetMap's own credit uses the copyright sign; this
           repository's text is ASCII only, so it is written (c) here.

Ours       tiny.map (a dozen junctions, made up by hand to follow a search
           step by step) and the made-up cities of gen.py's citymap kind
           (a seeded grid, for the growth harness) hold no OpenStreetMap
           data. They, route.c, gen.py and lib/roadgraph.c are this
           course's own, under the repository's MIT licence.
