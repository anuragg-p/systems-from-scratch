# 08.03 Dijkstra, with a heap

Dijkstra's algorithm finds the shortest route in metres, by finishing
junctions in order of their distance from the start. Each step needs the
nearest junction not finished yet; looking at every junction for it is
fine on central London and hopeless on a whole city, and the heap of 02.04
hands it over in a few comparisons instead.

## The idea

Breadth-first search took the tiny map's ring road from 3 to 8: 2 roads,
1200 metres, when the main street is 5 roads and 1000 metres. In London
the gap was 14480 metres against 12973. To find the fewest metres, the
search must spread by metres, not by roads: finish the junctions in order
of how far they are from FROM along the roads.

A queue cannot give that order: a junction one long road away is reached
early, but may be further than one three short roads away. So the search
keeps, for each junction reached, the shortest distance found so far, and
each step finishes the reached junction with the smallest distance, which
is now final. Then it follows that junction's roads, and where going that
way makes a neighbour nearer than it was, lowers the neighbour's distance
and notes the new way in. That check is **relaxing** the road; breadth-first
search never lowered a distance, and this search does it all the time.

The obvious way to find the nearest junction not finished is to look at
all of them, every step: `--algo=dijkstra-scan`. It is right, and costs n
looks per junction finished, 17260 each time on London.

The better way keeps the reached junctions in a **heap**, the structure of
02.04: a binary tree in an array, the smallest item always at the root, a
push or a pop in about log2 of its size steps. Ordered by distance, it has
the nearest junction waiting on top.

But the heap of 02.04 cannot change an item already in it: it has push,
pop, top and a length, and no way to find an entry, let alone lower it. So
when a junction's distance drops, the search pushes it again with the new
distance and leaves the old entry where it is. The new one is smaller and
comes off first; when the old one reaches the top, its junction is finished
already, and it is thrown away. That is **lazy deletion**: an entry is not
removed when it goes out of date, only skipped when it comes up.

![Dijkstra with a heap on tiny.map from 3 to 8: one frame per junction finished, each labelled with its distance in metres, and the heap's array under the map, out-of-date entries dashed](figures/08.03-heap.svg)

Junctions come off in order of metres: 3 at 0, 4 at 200, 2 at 300, 9 at
370, 5 at 400, then 0, 10, 1, 6, 7, 11 and 8. Junction 8 first gets 1200
from 1, by the ring road; when 7 is finished at 800 the main street makes
it 1000 and it is pushed again, and the entry at 1200 stays in the heap,
out of date, to the end. The route is the main street, 1000 metres; but
every junction was finished to find it, those west and north of 3 too.

### `search_dijkstra_scan`

The slow way, kept beside the fast one as this track always does: each
step looks at all n junctions for the nearest one reached and not finished,
the smaller number on a tie.

### `search_dijkstra`

The same loop with the heap. What it pushes comes with the lesson: a
`Waiting` entry holds a junction, the distance it was pushed with and the
key the heap orders by; `by_key()` puts the smaller key first and, on a
tie, the smaller junction, as the scan does. The entries come from a pool
allocated once for every push the search can make, so a pointer the heap
holds never moves. The rest is for `--algo` and `DS_TRACE`.

## How it works

### Why a finished distance is final

The **invariant**: every finished junction has its true shortest distance,
and every reached junction has the shortest distance by a way that passes
only finished junctions before it. Take u, the reached junction with the
smallest distance d. Any other way from FROM to u has to leave the
finished junctions somewhere, at a reached junction w, whose distance is
at least d, because u was the smallest; the roads from w on to u add
nothing negative. So no way to u is shorter than d, and u can be finished.
Relaxing u's roads then keeps the second half true. The argument needs
lengths that are never negative, which a road's never is.

The code keeps it by finishing only what comes off the top, and by never
changing a finished junction:

```
if (state[v] != FINISHED && (dist[v] < 0 || d < dist[v])) {
```

### Skipping what is out of date

```
if (state[u] == FINISHED) {
    stale++;
    if (tracing)
        trace_stale("dijkstra", e, &h);
    continue;
}
```

An entry for a junction already finished is out of date: a smaller one for
the same junction came off before it. Without this check the route is
still right, since relaxing a finished junction's roads again improves
nothing, but every out-of-date entry finishes its junction again. On the
crossing that makes 17166 junctions finished out of 15585 reached, and
46861 roads relaxed instead of 41480: more finished than reached is the
giveaway.

### How many entries, at most

A push happens only when a road is followed from a junction just
finished, and each junction is finished once, so each road is followed at
most twice, once from each end: at most 2m pushes, plus one for FROM, the
size of the pool. So a push or a pop takes up to log2 of 2m+1 steps rather
than log2 of n: on London 15.5 instead of 14.1. That, and the out-of-date
pops, is all lazy deletion costs.

## Decide the boundary

route keeps the heap of 02.04 as it is and pays in out-of-date entries:
1674 of 17166 pops on the crossing. Another design gives the heap a
**decrease-key**: it records where each junction's entry sits in the array,
updates that on every move, and sifts an entry up when its key drops, as
the indexed heap of Sedgewick and Wayne's Dijkstra does. Then it never
holds more than n entries or pops one for nothing, at the price of an
array of n positions and a heap that knows what its items are, which freq
and sort do not need. Would you change the one heap every tool shares, or let
this tool carry a few extra entries?

## Break it

Order the heap by junction number instead of distance. In
`build/at/dsa/08.03/tools/route/route.c`, in `by_key()`, delete the two
lines that compare the keys, leaving only the tie-break:
