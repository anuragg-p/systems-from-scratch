What learned positions are worth to a small GPT, block by block

Written by experiments/paper.py from the logs of experiments/recorded/pilot and
experiments/recorded/depth: runs on one laptop's CPU.

Abstract

Without position embeddings a GPT of one block cannot tell one order of its window from another, and
from the second block on its causal mask lets order in; at 4 blocks learned positions were worth
0.065 bits per character in an earlier run, at one learning rate for both models. We measure what
they are worth at 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8 blocks of a character-level GPT 64 wide on Tiny Shakespeare, each
model's learning rate chosen by the same search on held-out text, each depth on 22 pairs of runs
that share their batches and starting weights, a number fixed by a pilot before the experiment ran.
The gap falls from 0.288 bits (0.278 to 0.298) at one block to 0.124 bits (0.113 to 0.135) at 8 (95%
intervals), its interval stays above 0 at every depth, and no depth without positions reaches even
one block with them (3.384 bits at best, against 3.196). At 4 blocks it is 0.201, 3.1 times the
earlier run's, because the model with positions gains most from a larger rate; and the weights
positions add, given to the model without them, close none of it (+0.004 bits).

1. The problem

Take 32 characters of Shakespeare and a GPT of one block without position embeddings, and put the 31
characters before the last in another order. Its scores for the next character do not move: in the
earlier run the largest change over five orders was 3.55e-15, rounding. With four blocks they moved
by up to 2.44. In one block the last position weighs a set of characters, and a weighted sum does
not care in what order its terms come; from the second block on, each position reads what the first
block saw at the positions up to it, and the causal mask made those views differ. So a GPT without
positions sees some of the order, and at four blocks learned positions were worth only 0.065 bits
per character (3.407 against 3.342, 3 seeds). How much are they worth as blocks are added, and how
many blocks does a GPT without them need to match one with them?

2. What this paper shows

- The gap at 1, 2, 4, 6, 8 blocks, each on 22 pairs of runs, each model's rate searched alike
  (section 4): 0.288 bits (0.278 to 0.298) at one block, 0.124 bits (0.113 to 0.135) at 8; at no
  depth tried does its 95% interval reach 0.
- How many blocks without positions match one with them: none of 1 to 8. The best without, 8 blocks,
  scores 3.384 bits; the worst with, 8 blocks, 3.260 (section 4).
- The rate matters more than the depth: at 0.003, the earlier run's rate, the search put the gap at
  4 blocks at 0.076 on validation, at the rates it chose at 0.169 (sections 4 and 6).
- Not the weights: the 2048 weights positions add at 4 blocks, put into the MLPs of the model
  without them instead, close none of it (+0.004 bits) (section 4).
- Seven predictions written before any run: 6 held (section 5).

3. Method

Model. The GPT of the track's module 08: pre-norm blocks of attention and an MLP, GELU, a last layer
norm, the output tied to the embeddings, GPT-2's start (every weight drawn with sd 0.02); width 64,
4 heads, windows of 32 characters, 1 to 8 blocks. With learned positions it has a table of 32 x 64 =
2048 more weights, added to the embeddings.

Data. Tiny Shakespeare, 1,115,394 bytes, one byte a character: the first 90% to train on, the next
5% to choose on (validation), the last 5% to report on (test), split by position. Scores are bits
per character, each character read with 16 or more before it.

Training. AdamW, weight decay 0.1 on the matrices and embeddings, the rate warmed up over 50 steps
then a cosine to 0, the gradient clipped to 1.0; 300 steps of 32 windows.

Search. At every depth both models ran at each of the rates 0.0015, 0.003, 0.006, 0.012 on seed 0,
and each kept the rate with the lowest validation bits.

Pairs. Seeds 1 to 22 at the rates chosen: one seed gives both models the same batches and the same
start for every weight they share; only the table of positions is not shared.

Seeds. A pilot before the experiment ran 4 pairs at 1 block and 8 blocks, rate 0.003, scored on
validation only. Its largest standard deviation of a depth's gaps, 0.0315, and the smallest gap
worth finding, 0.02 bits, ask for 22 pairs for a true gap of that size to come out clear of 0 four
times in five.

Control. At 4 blocks the model without positions again, with every MLP 4 hidden units wider (2064
weights, as many as positions add, within a few), at the rate chosen for the model without
positions, on the same seeds.

Statistics. Each pair's gap, no positions minus learned; the mean with a 95% paired t interval, and
Welch's interval on the same runs beside it.

Cost. The pilot took 157 s (3 minutes) and the experiment 2817 s (47 minutes) on one laptop's CPU,
with other work running.

5. The predictions, written before any run

one_block: Positions are worth the most to a GPT of one block, which cannot see order at all without
them.
   rule: held if the mean gap at 1 block is the largest
   ours: 0.288 (the mean gap at 1 block against the mean gap at every other depth)
   held

shrinks: The gap shrinks as blocks are added: the mask gives a deeper model more of the order.
   rule: held if the mean gap falls at every step of depth
   ours: 0.288, 0.271, 0.201, 0.141, 0.124 (the mean gap at each depth against the one before it)
   held

size_one: At one block the gap is more than 0.1 bits per character.
   rule: held if the gap's interval at 1 block is all above 0.1
   ours: 0.288, 0.278, 0.298 (the gap at 1 block, its paired interval)
   held

again: At four blocks, each arm with its own searched rate, the gap is about 08.03's 0.065.
   rule: held if the mean gap at 4 blocks is within 0.0325 to 0.13
   ours: 0.201 (the mean gap at 4 blocks, within a factor of 2 of 0.065)
   did not hold

not_closed: No depth tried closes the gap: at the deepest, positions still help beyond the noise.
   rule: held if the gap's interval at the deepest is all above 0
   ours: 0.124, 0.113, 0.135 (the gap at the deepest depth, its paired interval)
   held

depth_no_substitute: Twice the blocks do not buy what positions buy: no positions at 8 blocks is
worse than learned positions at 4.
   rule: held if Welch's interval (none at the deepest, learned at 4 blocks) is above 0
   ours: 0.193, 0.183, 0.202 (test bits without positions at the deepest depth minus with them at 4,
   Welch's interval)
   held

not_weights: The positions' weights are not why they help: the same number of weights given to the
MLPs of a GPT without positions closes under a quarter of the gap.
   rule: held if it closes under 25% of the gap at 4 blocks
   ours: -0.018 (the share of the gap at 4 blocks the wider model without positions closes, by the
   means)
   held

6. What was not expected

The predictions that did not hold: again (did not hold).

What follows was seen in the numbers after the runs; no rule was written for it before.

The rate, more than the depth. At 4 blocks the gap on validation (seed 0, the search) is 0.016 at
0.0015, 0.076 at 0.003, 0.167 at 0.006, 0.169 at 0.012. From the earlier run's 0.003 to the rate
each model's search chose, the model with positions gained 0.117 bits and the model without them
0.025. One rate for both, chosen for neither, measured 45% of the gap at the rates the searches
chose; the prediction 'again' failed for that reason.

Why the gap shrinks. With positions the best depth is 2 blocks (3.164 bits); from there to 8 blocks
the model with positions gets worse by 0.096, and the model without gets better by 0.051. Of the
gap's fall of 0.147, 65% is the deeper model with positions doing worse in 300 steps, not the deeper
model without them catching up: 'shrinks' held by its rule, and not for the reason it gave.

Pairing bought nothing. The paired intervals are 0.97 to 1.11 times as wide as Welch's on the same
runs: the two models of a seed do not move together (their correlation over seeds is -0.17 to
+0.15), though they share their batches and every weight but the table of positions.

Validation and test agree: at no depth do the two texts' gaps differ by more than 0.007 bits, so the
test text's own sampling is not what the numbers show.

7 of the 10 choices of the search are an end of its range (learned at 2 blocks, none at 2 blocks,
learned at 4 blocks, none at 4 blocks, none at 6 blocks, learned at 8 blocks, none at 8 blocks): a
search whose best is at its edge may have stopped short of the best.

A follow-up past the top of the search, at 4 blocks: both models at 0.012, 0.024, 0.048 on seed 0,
validation bits 3.379, 3.415, 3.867 without positions and 3.210, 3.235, 4.045 with them, so the
search keeps 0.012 and 0.012, the experiment's own: there its edge was the best rate, not a limit.
Its 3 pairs give a gap of 0.211 (0.133 to 0.290), against 0.201 in the experiment; run at the same
rates and seeds by another command, they are the experiment's seeds 1 to 3 again, to the last digit.
It took 96 s (2 minutes).

7. What others found

- Irie, Zeyer, Schlueter and Ney (2019), Language Modeling with Deep Transformers, Interspeech 2019,
  arXiv 1905.04226 (section 7.1 and table 9): word-level models of 12, 24 and 42 layers on
  LibriSpeech: without positions the perplexity is lower than with sinusoidal ones at every depth
  (development set 60.5 against 63.1 at 12 layers, 56.6 against 58.0 at 24, 54.2 against 55.0 at
  42); 'the positional encoding is not needed for multi-layer autoregressive self-attention models'.
- Haviv, Ram, Press, Izsak and Levy (2022), Transformer Language Models without Positional Encodings
  Still Learn Positional Information, Findings of EMNLP 2022, arXiv 2203.16634 (table 2 and section
  5): on the Pile, 125 million to 1.3 billion weights (12 to 24 layers): no positions against
  learned, perplexity 22.15 against 22.04 at 125M and 13.10 against 13.05 at 1.3B, 'these
  performance gaps diminish as the models scale up'; a probe finds the model without positions has
  no position in its first layer and 'becomes position-aware within four layers'.
- Kazemnejad, Padhi, Natesan Ramamurthy, Das and Reddy (2023), The Impact of Positional Encoding on
  Length Generalization in Transformers, NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2305.19466 (theorem 1): 'the first
  layer of f_theta can recover absolute positions' (with a beginning-of-sequence token at the start
  of every input), so that later layers can use them; models without positions generalise to longer
  inputs better than with any explicit positions, on reasoning and arithmetic tasks.
- Chi, Fan, Chen, Rudnicky and Ramadge (2023), Latent Positional Information is in the Self-
  Attention Variance of Transformer Language Models Without Positional Embeddings, ACL 2023, arXiv
  2305.13571 (the abstract): a model without positions, even untrained, 'inherently encodes strong
  positional information through the shrinkage of self-attention variance': the average over more
  earlier positions varies less.
- Zuo, Guerzhoy and Guerzhoy (2024), Position Information Emerges in Causal Transformers Without
  Positional Encodings via Similarity of Nearby Embeddings, COLING 2025, arXiv 2501.00073 (the
  abstract): 'nearby embeddings are more similar to each other than faraway embeddings, allowing the
  transformer to potentially reconstruct the positions of tokens', in trained and untrained models.

Not found in that search: the gap block by block from one block up, in a small model, at a fixed
budget. The search was a few queries of the web and arXiv and the papers they led to, not a survey.

8. Limits

- One size (64 wide, 4 heads, windows of 32), one text, one budget (300 steps): a model trained
  longer, or wider, or on another text may give another curve.
- Each rate was chosen on one seed; a rate that was lucky on seed 0 runs on every later seed.
- The windows start at random places in the text and have no token that marks a start, which the
  proof of Kazemnejad et al. uses.
- The runs record their settings and seeds, not the machine or the library's version; the last
  digits can differ on another machine.
