# 06.03 A convolutional network on CIFAR-10

Handwritten digits are an easy start. This lesson moves to photographs:
CIFAR-10, sixty thousand small colour pictures of ten kinds of thing,
read straight from the dataset's own files of bytes. On 2000 of them it
trains the fully connected network, then 06.02's convolutional one, then
the same convolutional network on a new crop and mirror image of every
picture at every pass, and compares how much of what each one learned
holds on pictures it never saw.

## Where this fits

![Where 06.03 fits](figures/06.03-map.svg)

This lesson's piece is in the data row: CIFAR-10, fetched once by an
explicit command and read with numpy, beside MNIST. The augmentation box
next to it gains crops and mirror images for colour pictures. Above them,
the experiment trains three networks with a training loop written once
for every image classifier from here on, on PyTorch, keeping the data
where the network computes. After this lesson the track trains and
scores networks on real photographs, and 06.04's residual networks use
the same data, loop and augmentation.

## The idea

**CIFAR-10** is 60,000 colour images of 32 by 32 pixels in ten classes,
6000 of each: airplane, automobile, bird, cat, deer, dog, frog, horse,
ship and truck. 50,000 are for training and 10,000 for testing.
Krizhevsky (2009) made it by paying students to check, class by class,
small pictures found on the web by searching for the class's name, and
the dataset's page says that
"The classes are completely mutually exclusive. There is no overlap
between automobiles and trucks." A digit is a few strokes on a blank
background; a frog here can be any size, anywhere in the picture, in any
light, on any background.

![CIFAR-10's first training record, as data_batch_1.bin holds it: a label byte, then 1024 bytes of red, 1024 of green, 1024 of blue, each 32 rows of 32](figures/06.03-record.svg)

The track fetches the dataset's binary version: six files of records,
five for training and one for testing, 10,000 records each. A record is
one byte for the label, 0 to 9, then the picture's 3072 bytes: the red
channel row by row, then the green, then the blue. Nothing marks where a
record ends; each is exactly 3073 bytes long. Reading one is a reshape:
the file's bytes in rows of 3073, the first column the labels, the rest
the pictures as arrays of shape (3, 32, 32). The figure is the first
record of the first file, a frog.

Three ways to learn the ten classes from the first 2000 training
pictures, each for 12 passes of SGD with momentum, three seeds each, all
scored on 2000 test pictures:

1. The obvious thing: the fully connected network, every one of the
   3072 numbers of a picture an input, 256 hidden units, ten scores.
   789,258 weights.
2. 06.02's convolutional network, 16 filters wide: three convolutions of
   16, 32 and 64 filters, each with relu and pooling, and a Linear layer.
   33,834 weights, a twenty-third as many.
3. The same convolutional network, shown at every pass a new crop and
   mirror image of every training picture.

The third is the data augmentation of 05.03 for photographs, as He et
al. (2015) train their residual networks on CIFAR-10: "4 pixels are
padded on each side, and a 32x32 crop is randomly sampled from the
padded image or its horizontal flip." A crop moves the picture by up to
4 pixels each way, as 05.03's shifts moved digits; a mirror image swaps
left and right, which keeps the label of a photograph (a horse facing
left is still a horse) where it would not keep a digit's.

![CIFAR-10's first training image, a frog, padded by 4 pixels of the average colour, and four crops and mirror images crop_flip made of it (the mean of the three channels drawn)](figures/06.03-crops.svg)

### The download

`data/fetch.py` gains CIFAR-10 beside MNIST, and `fetch_archive()` for a
dataset that comes as one archive. CIFAR-10's binary version is one
archive of 170,052,171 bytes on the dataset's page, which is slow and
drops connections, so the download carries on where it stopped, five
times at most. The archive must have the size and SHA-256 written in
`data/fetch.py`; then the seven files the track uses are taken out of it
by name, each checked the same way, and the archive is deleted. Nothing
else in the track downloads anything.

### `read_records`

One file of records as numpy arrays: its bytes, refused unless they are
a whole number of 3073-byte records, then the labels and the pictures.

### `load`

A split, training or test, the first n records when n is given, reading
only as many files as that needs: pixels scaled to 0..1, then each
channel standardised with the training set's own mean and standard
deviation, which `data/cifar.py` writes down, as 03.01 did for MNIST with
one channel. Without the files it stops the script with one line that
names the fetch command.

### `crop_flip`

The augmentation: every picture padded with 4 pixels of zeros on each
side, a 32 by 32 window cut out of it at its own random offset, and that
window mirrored left to right for a random half of the pictures, all
drawn from the run's generator, all pictures at once by indexing as in
05.03.

### `train_epoch`

One pass over a training set that is already tensors on the run's
device: a new order of batches from the run's generator, and for each
batch the cross-entropy, `zero_grad()`, `backward()`, the optimiser's
step, and a learning rate scheduler's step when there is one (06.04 has
one). It returns the pass's mean loss and the fraction it got right, as
it went.

### `evaluate`

The loss and the accuracy of a model on a set, in eval mode, without a
graph, in batches of 1000; the model goes back to training mode after.

### `train`

One of the three ways, one seed: the network, seeded just before it is
built, SGD with momentum at 0.01 for the fully connected network and 0.02
for the convolutional one (the rates that did best for each in a short
sweep made before this page was written), and every pass on the pictures
as they are or on a new `crop_flip()` of them, logged pass by pass.

**The fully connected network memorises.** It gets 96.2% of its 2000
training pictures right and 38.0% of the test pictures: the **gap**,
training accuracy minus test accuracy, is 0.582. Chance is 10%, so it
learned something that holds, and a great deal that does not. With a
weight for every number of every pixel, it can fit 2000 pictures by
their details, and it does.

**The convolutional network generalises better, and memorises less.**
With a twenty-third of the weights it gets 45.5% of the test pictures,
against 38.0% (t = +8.1), and its gap is 0.344. Its filters see the
same pattern wherever it is in the picture, as 06.01 measured on moved
digits, and there are far fewer weights to fit details with.

**Crops and flips: here, 3 points.** On a new crop and mirror image of
every picture at every pass, the same network fits its training pictures
far less, 56.4%, and gets 48.6% of the test pictures, against 45.5%
(t = +3.0). The gap closes from 0.344 to 0.078, from both sides, as
05.03's shifts closed it on digits.

## How it works

### Bytes, not pickles

```
    raw = np.fromfile(filename, dtype=np.uint8)
    if raw.size % RECORD:
```

CIFAR-10 also comes as Python pickles, which most code online loads.
Unpickling a file runs whatever code the file asks for, so a pickle
from the network is a program from the network. The binary version is
only bytes: `np.fromfile` reads them as unsigned 8-bit numbers, one per
byte, and a reshape gives the records. The only thing to check is that
the file is a whole number of records, which a file cut short or the
wrong file would not be. The fetch has already checked every file's
SHA-256 against the one written in the project, so the bytes are the
ones these lessons were run on.

### One copy of the pictures a pass

```
    for epoch in range(cfg.epochs):
        (images,) = tensors(dev, crop_flip(x, 4, run.rng) if augment else x)
```

The pictures stay numpy arrays until they are used. Each pass turns the
whole training set, cropped and flipped anew or as it is, into one
tensor on the run's device, and `train_epoch()` picks each batch out of
it by position. On a GPU that is one copy a pass instead of one a batch.
For the same reason `train_epoch()` adds up its loss and its count of
right answers as tensors and turns them into Python numbers once, at the
end of the pass: `.item()` makes the CPU wait for the device, and once
a pass is cheaper than once a batch.

### Zeros are the average colour

```
    padded = np.pad(x, ((0, 0), (0, 0), (pad, pad), (pad, pad)))
```

05.03 padded digits with the value of a blank pixel, standardised, so
that what moved in at the edge was white like the background. A
photograph has no background colour. These pictures are standardised
channel by channel, so 0 is the training set's average red, green and
blue: the crops bring in a border of the average colour, the grey band
in the figure above, and no edge of a colour the pictures do not have.

## Decide the boundary

This track keeps the whole training set in memory as one array and
copies it to the device as one tensor. CIFAR-10's 50,000 training
pictures are 614 MB as float32 (50,000 times 3072 numbers of 4 bytes),
which a laptop holds. A dataset of millions of larger pictures does not
fit; there each batch is read from disk and decoded while the previous
one trains, by worker processes (PyTorch's `DataLoader`, which the track
does not use). That costs code and moving parts, and it keeps memory
flat whatever the dataset's size. When does a dataset stop fitting?

## Break it

A mirror image keeps the label of a photograph. Turn the pictures upside
down instead: in `build/at/research/06.03/data/augment.py`, in
`crop_flip()`, flip the rows instead of the columns:

<!-- verify: patch file=data/augment.py

-->
```
    out[flip] = out[flip][:, :, ::-1, :]
```

The first two rows do not use the augmentation and are unchanged. The
third falls to 39.2% on test, below the network with no augmentation at
all (t = -3.2), and the gap is small for the wrong reason: the network
now does badly on both. An upside-down ship is still labelled a ship,
but no test picture is upside down, and half of what the network was
shown is pictures of a kind it will never be asked about; skies at the
bottom, wheels in the air. Every augmentation is a claim that a change
keeps the label and the kind of picture, and this one keeps only the
first. Put `::-1` back on the columns.

## Go deeper

- [The CIFAR-10 dataset](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~kriz/cifar.html) by Alex Krizhevsky - the ten classes with samples of each, the split into five training batches and one test batch, and the binary version's format, record by record, that `read_records()` reads.
- [Learning Multiple Layers of Features from Tiny Images](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~kriz/learning-features-2009-TR.pdf) by Alex Krizhevsky, 2009 - the report the dataset's page asks you to cite; section 3.1, "The labeled subset", for how the pictures were found and labelled, the rules the labellers followed, and the removal of near-duplicates.
- [Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385) by He, Zhang, Ren and Sun, 2015 - section 4.2 for the padding, crop and flip used here and the per-pixel mean subtracted first; 06.04 runs that section's experiment.
