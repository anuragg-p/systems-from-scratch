# Module 07 - Language models, from counting to attention

Module 06 classified pictures; this module guesses the next character of a text, a million characters of Shakespeare shipped with the project, and scores every guess by how surprised it was: bits per character on text the model never saw, the cross-entropy of 02.04 in base 2. Counting pairs of characters sets the ladder every later model has to beat: 6.022 bits for a blind guess, 4.850 for single characters, 3.599 for pairs, and Shannon's own figures for English beside ours. Counting with longer contexts then fails as Bengio et al. said it would: most contexts are never seen, and the held-out score turns up past four characters however the counts are smoothed. Their network, which gives each character a learned vector and reads the context's vectors, is far ahead of plain smoothed counting in a minute's run, still 0.058 bits behind the paper's own baseline; a recorded run of 20 minutes beats that baseline at 3, 5 and 8 characters in every seed. A recurrent network, written by hand and checked against PyTorch's, reads any length of text into one state, and the module measures how it forgets: nothing more than 16 characters back changes its guess, a symbol is recalled after 10 blanks and lost by 40, and the gradient that would teach it to remember shrinks by orders of magnitude on the way back. Last, Bahdanau et al.'s attention: an encoder and decoder that copy a sequence through one vector fall to near chance by 20 symbols, and with a decoder that looks back at every position the copy holds. It stands on module 02's softmax and cross-entropy, module 03's Adam and schedule, module 05's three sets, and module 06's PyTorch conventions. After it the track has a text, a measure, a ladder of models to beat, and attention as a weighted sum that the next module turns inward, onto one sequence.
