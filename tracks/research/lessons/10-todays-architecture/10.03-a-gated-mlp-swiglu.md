# 10.03 A gated MLP: SwiGLU

08.02's MLP takes each position's vector up to four times its width,
bends every number with GELU, and brings it back down. Shazeer (2020)
replaced the first layer and its bend with a gate: two layers up, one of
them bent and used to scale the other number by number. That is three
matrices where the MLP had two, so he made the inner width two thirds as
wide to keep the weights the same, and found it better by a clear margin,
with, in his words, no explanation. This lesson builds it, counts the
weights, and finds that what a comparison says depends on how long it
runs: after the page's 150 steps SwiGLU is behind, beyond the noise; in
10.05's recorded long run it is ahead by 0.067 bits, the largest single
change of the module.

## Where this fits

![Where 10.03 fits](figures/10.03-map.svg)

This lesson's piece is today's architecture again, in the models row:

## The idea

### What 08's MLP does, and costs

08.02's MLP is `up`, a Linear layer from `width` to `4 x width` numbers,
GELU on each of them, and `down`, a Linear layer back. At the page's width
of 64, with biases, that is 64 x 256 + 256 weights up and 256 x 64 + 64
down: 33,088 a block, 132,352 in the four, nearly two thirds of 08.04's
GPT. GELU is a fixed bend: every
inner number goes through the same curve, about the number itself when it
is large, about 0 when it is very negative.

### A gate

Dauphin, Fan, Auli and Grangier (2017) built a **gated linear unit**:
two linear layers of the same input, one of them put through a sigmoid
(04.03), so that it lies between 0 and 1, and the two multiplied number by
number: (xW + b) times sigmoid(xV + c), their equation 1. The sigmoid side
is a gate on each number of the other side: shut near 0, open near 1,
decided by the input itself. Their reason, from their section 1: "a
linear path for the gradients while retaining non-linear capabilities".

Shazeer's "GLU Variants Improve Transformer" (2020) puts gated units in
the Transformer's MLP and tries other bends for the gate. One of them is
**Swish**, x times sigmoid(x), which PyTorch calls **SiLU**: SwiGLU is
SiLU(xW) times xV, then a third layer back down, all three without
biases (his equation 6). SiLU is close to GELU in shape, the gate in it
being the input's own sigmoid; the difference is the second layer, `up`,
whose numbers the gate scales.

### The same weights

"All of these layers have three weight matrices, as opposed to two for
the original FFN. To keep the number of parameters and the amount of
computation constant, we reduce the number of hidden units ... by a
factor of 2/3" (his section 2). His model, a T5 of 12 layers each side
and width 768, has an inner width of 3072 for the plain MLP and 2048 for
the gated ones. Here the inner width is 8 x width / 3, rounded down: 170
at a width of 64, and 1536, exactly, at SmolLM2's 576 (10.04).

![08.02's MLP beside the gated MLP, at the page's width of 64 with biases: two matrices against three, the inner width cut to two thirds so the weights match (33,088 against 33,044); SiLU, the gate's bend, beside GELU](figures/10.03-mlp.svg)

His claim is his table 1, the log-perplexity of the held-out text (the
logarithm of 08.05's perplexity: a loss, lower is better) after 65,536
steps, the mean of four runs: GELU's MLP 1.983, SwiGLU 1.944, a
gain of 0.039 against a spread of 0.005 to 0.010; after 524,288 steps,
1.679 against 1.636. And his section 4: "We offer no explanation as to
why these architectures seem to work; we attribute their success, as all
else, to divine benevolence."

### What the run does

1. The weights of one MLP, 08's and SwiGLU's, at the page's width and at
   SmolLM2's, with biases and without.
2. 08.04's GPT with each, 150 steps, 3 seeds: bits per character on the
   test text, ms a step, weights.
3. What the gates of the trained model do: in each block, how many of the
   inner numbers the gate shuts and how many it opens wide.

### `SwiGLU`

Three Linear layers, `gate` and `up` from `width` to `hidden` and `down`
back; SiLU of the gate's numbers times up's, then down.

### `gates`

For each block of a trained GPT, the share of SiLU(gate) under 0.1 in
size, nearly shut, and over 1, open wider than up's own number.

**The weights match.** At width 64 with biases the gated MLP has 44
weights fewer a block, 176 in the model: two thirds of 256 is 170.7, and
the width is rounded down. Without biases, 128 fewer. At SmolLM2's width
of 576 without biases, the two are exactly equal, 2,654,208: 576 is a
multiple of 3. The multiplies a position costs follow the weights, so the
time of a step should be about the same, and it was: within the machine's
noise in every run made for this module.

**Behind, at 150 steps.** 3.596 bits per character against the GELU
MLP's 3.563, t = +4.7: beyond the noise, the wrong way. Every SwiGLU seed
is worse than every GELU seed. A learner who stopped here would report
that Shazeer's change hurts.

**The gates shut, a lot.** In the first block a quarter of the inner
numbers are nearly shut for any one character, 0.262, and 6% are open
wider than up's own number. In the three blocks after it about two
fifths are shut, 0.401 to 0.425, and almost none wide open. Which ones
depends on the character and what came before it: each position switches
off its own two fifths of the MLP.

### Ahead, once trained a while

The same script with more steps, at the same size and the same three
seeds, is the question the 150 steps cannot answer:

<!-- verify: skip reason="two runs of several minutes each, made for this page's figure; their numbers are quoted below" -->
```
$ python3 -m experiments.swiglu steps=600
$ python3 -m experiments.swiglu steps=1200
```

They took a few minutes each on this laptop's CPU, with other work
running. At 600 steps SwiGLU is ahead: 2.962 bits per character against
the GELU MLP's 3.020, -0.058, t = -3.4. At 1200, 2.673 against 2.725,
-0.053, t = -3.9. Somewhere between 150 and 600 steps the sign turns,
and once turned it stays. 10.05's short run, 80 steps, has it further
behind still, +0.111. In 10.05's recorded long run, a model of width 128
trained for 2000 steps, SwiGLU is 0.067 bits ahead on the test text, t =
-10.9, the largest single change of the module. And there, wider and at
a lower rate, it is ahead on the validation windows from the first logged
step after the start, 100: what slows it at width 64 is not something
every model shows.

![SwiGLU behind early and ahead later: its bits per character on the test text less the GELU MLP's, at width 64 for 80 to 1200 steps (3 seeds; 2 at 80; the whisker one spread of the difference either side); and in 10.05's recorded run, width 128, on validation every 100 steps](figures/10.03-budget.svg)

Why it starts slower is a reason offered, not a measurement. At the
start every weight of `gate` and `up` is small, drawn with a standard
deviation of 0.02, so each of their numbers is small; SiLU of a small
number is about half of it; and the gated MLP's output is the product of
two small numbers, smaller again, where GELU's MLP passes one small
number through. So the gated MLP would add almost nothing to the stream
until its weights have grown, and the model would spend its first steps
on attention alone. The page's run does not test this, and the recorded
run, ahead from step 100, says it is at most part of the story.

Ours beside the paper's:

| | Shazeer (2020) | the page's run, and 10.05's |
|---|---|---|
| the weights | matched, an inner width of 2048 for 3072 | matched: 170 for 256 at width 64 (176 fewer in the model), 1536 for 2304 at 576 (equal) |
| the gain | log-perplexity 1.944 against 1.983 at 65,536 steps, sd 0.010 and 0.005 over 4 runs | +0.033 bits per character (behind) at 150 steps; -0.058 at 600 and -0.053 at 1200; -0.067 at 2000 steps of a wider model |
| why | "divine benevolence" | a reason offered for the slow start, not tested |

What held: the gain, once the model has trained a while: 0.067 bits per
character in the long run beside his 0.039 of log-perplexity per token,
two measures on two tasks that do not compare directly. What did not: at the page's budget the change is a loss, and a
comparison stopped there would say the paper was wrong. A change can
cost early and pay late, and a short run measures only the cost.

## How it works

### One line

```
        return self.down(F.silu(self.gate(x)) * self.up(x))
```

`self.gate(x)` and `self.up(x)` are both (..., hidden); `F.silu` bends
the first and `*` multiplies the two number by number, so each inner
number of `up` is scaled by its own gate, a different gate for every
position. Remove the `*` and its other side and the MLP is two Linear
layers in a row, which is one linear map: everything nonlinear in this
MLP is in that product and that bend.

### Two thirds, rounded down

```
        self.mlp = SwiGLU(width, hidden or 8 * width // 3, bias) if mlp == "swiglu" else MLP(width, bias)
```

`8 * width // 3` is two thirds of four times the width, rounded down:
170 for 64, 1536 for 576. `hidden=` overrides it, which 10.04 needs:
SmolLM2's file has an inner width of 1536 and nothing to compute it from.

### The start, by name

```
                into_stream = name.endswith("attention.out.weight") or name.endswith("mlp.down.weight")
```

`GPT.__init__` starts every weight by its name, as GPT-2 does. SwiGLU's
layer back into the stream is called `down`, like the MLP's, so it is
started by sqrt(2 x layers) smaller, as the other layers that add into the
stream are (08.02). `gate` and `up` start as every other weight, sd 0.02.
The names are chosen so that the start, and 10.04's loading, need nothing
new.

## Decide the boundary

Shazeer measured a gain and offered no reason for it, and every large
open model since has taken the change. This lesson found the gain only
past the page's budget, at one size, with a reason for the slow start
that is a guess. Ablations like this one say whether a change pays; they
do not say why, and a change that pays for unknown reasons may stop
paying at a size or a budget nobody tried. When do you put a change into
your model on a measured gain alone, and what would you run before
trusting it at a size you cannot afford to test?
