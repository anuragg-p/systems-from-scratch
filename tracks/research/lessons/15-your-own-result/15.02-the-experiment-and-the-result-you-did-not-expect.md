# 15.02 The experiment, and the result you did not expect

15.01's plan, run: 08.03's GPT at 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8 blocks, without
positions and with learned positions, each model's learning rate found by
the same search, 22 pairs of seeds at every depth, 47 minutes. Positions
are worth most at one block, 0.288 bits per character, and less with
every block added, 0.124 at eight, and no depth without them reaches even
one block with them. Six of the seven predictions held. The one that did
not is the finding: at four blocks positions are worth 0.201 bits, three
times 08.03's 0.065, because the model with positions gains far more from
a larger learning rate than the model without them, and 08.03 gave both
one rate. And one prediction that held did so for another reason than the
one it gave.

## The idea

### The obvious experiment, and what is wrong with it

The obvious way to answer 15.01's question is 08.03's script with the
number of blocks changed: both models at 08.03's rate, 0.003, three seeds
each, compared by Welch's t. The recorded pilot ran nearly that at one and
eight blocks, and found positions worth 0.149 bits at one and 0.032 at
eight. Three things are wrong with it, and module 13 named each:

- **One rate, chosen for neither.** 0.003 was 08.03's choice for one model
  at one depth. The Deep Learning Tuning Playbook calls a setting like the
  rate a nuisance: "The learning rate is a nuisance hyperparameter because
  we can only fairly compare models with different numbers of hidden layers
  if the learning rate is tuned separately for each number of layers (the
  optimal learning rate generally depends on the model architecture)." Here
  the two models differ too, so each needs its own search, the same search
  (13.01).
- **Three seeds.** The pilot's spread at one block asks for 22 pairs to
  see a gap of 0.02 (15.01).
- **Unpaired runs.** Two sets of runs compared by Welch's t carry
  everything a seed does to both models as noise; pairs leave it out
  (13.02).

So the experiment, as 15.01 fixed it before it ran:

1. **The search.** At each depth, both models at each of four rates,
   0.0015, 0.003, 0.006 and 0.012, on seed 0, scored on the validation
   text only. Each model keeps, at each depth, the rate with the lowest
   validation bits.
2. **The pairs.** Seeds 1 to 22, fresh: each model at its chosen rate, the
   two of a seed built by `pair`, scored on the validation text and then on
   the test text.
3. **The control.** At four blocks, the model without positions given as
   many weights as positions add, 2048, in its MLPs, on the same seeds.

40 runs of search, 220 of pairs and 22 of the control: 282 runs.

### `search`

The same rates for both models at every depth, on one seed, each run's
rate written in its config.json and its validation bits in its
summary.json; never the test text.

### `choose`

Each model's rate at each depth: the one with the lowest validation bits.
It reads nothing else, so nothing about the test text can move a choice.

### `Wider`

The control's MLP: a block's own MLP, copied, with a few hidden units more
that start as GPT-2's weights start. Four more units in each of four blocks
is 2064 weights, against the 2048 of the table of positions. If positions
helped only by being more weights, the model without positions given as
many would close the gap.

### `gaps`

At each depth, each pair's gap and their mean, the paired 95% interval
(13.02's `difference` with `paired=True`), Welch's interval on the same
runs, and on how many pairs positions were ahead.

**Two pairs decide little.** The page's own run finds positions ahead on
both of its pairs at both depths, 0.0999 bits at one block and 0.0581 at
two, and neither interval excludes 0: with two pairs the t of a 95%
interval is 12.7. Its verdicts are a small run's: `size_one` not settled,
and `not_closed`, judged at its deepest, two blocks, did not hold. It
shows the method; the 22 pairs of the record are the result.

**The search.** Most choices fall at the largest rate tried, 0.012: 7 of
the 10. At one block both models keep 0.006, and with positions at one
block 0.012 is far worse (3.4451 against 3.2326). Without positions the
rate matters little past 0.003; with positions it matters a great deal.

![The search, seed 0: validation bits per character against the learning rate at every depth, without positions (dashed) and with learned positions (solid), a dot at the rate each kept](figures/15.02-search.svg)

**The gap: 0.288 at one block, 0.124 at eight, every pair.** On all 22
pairs at every depth the model with positions is lower. The gap falls at
every step: 0.288, 0.271, 0.201, 0.141 and 0.124 bits per character, each
with a paired 95% interval 0.010 to 0.015 either side. It is still 0.113 at
least at eight blocks: no depth tried closes it. On the validation text
the gaps are within 0.007 of these.

![Test bits per character against blocks, 22 pairs of seeds, each model at its chosen rate: the mean and the seeds' range; rings: 08.03's two runs at 4 blocks](figures/15.02-bits.svg)

**No depth without positions reaches one block with them.** The best
model without positions, eight blocks, scores 3.384 bits; the worst with
them, also eight blocks, 3.260, and one block with them 3.196. The
question's second half, how many blocks a GPT without positions needs to
match one with them, has the answer "more than eight", at this size and
budget: depth adds less to it than one table of positions does.

![The gap, no positions minus learned, against blocks: each pair (small dots), the mean, its paired 95% interval (solid bar) and Welch's on the same runs (dashed, to its right); the ring: 08.03's 0.065](figures/15.02-gap.svg)

**Not the weights.** The control, the model without positions at four
blocks with 2064 more weights in its MLPs (the table of positions is
2048), scores 3.396 bits against 3.392 without them: it closes none of
the gap, and is 0.004 bits worse, inside the noise.

**Six of seven held.** Each prediction by the rule written before any run:

![Each prediction, written before any run, beside its outcome by its own rule](figures/15.02-verdicts.svg)

### What was not expected

**`again` did not hold: the rate, more than the depth.** At four blocks
positions are worth 0.201 bits, not about 0.065. 08.03's number was not
wrong: it was a number at one rate, 0.003, for both models. In the
search, at four blocks, the gap on validation is 0.016 at 0.0015, 0.076
at 0.003, 0.167 at 0.006 and 0.169 at 0.012. From 0.003 to the rate each
search chose, the model with positions gained 0.117 bits and the model
without them 0.025. A rate chosen for neither measured 45% of the gap
the searched rates show. Why the model with positions wants the larger
step in 300 steps, these runs do not say.

![From the search, seed 0: the gap on the validation text against the learning rate, one line per depth; 08.03 ran at 0.003](figures/15.02-rate.svg)

**`shrinks` held, for another reason than it gave.** The prediction said
the gap shrinks because the mask gives a deeper model more of the order.
The model without positions does get better with depth, 3.435 bits at two
blocks to 3.384 at eight, by 0.051. But the model with positions gets
worse: its best is two blocks, 3.164, and it is 3.260 at eight, 0.096
worse. Of the gap's fall from two blocks to eight, 0.147, 65% is the
deeper model with positions doing worse in 300 steps. A rule about
numbers held; the story attached to it did not.

**Pairing bought nothing.** 13.02's heads were paired and their paired
interval was 26% narrower than Welch's. Here the two are the same width,
0.97 to 1.11 times Welch's: the two models of a seed share their batches
and every weight but the table, and over seeds they do not move together
(correlation -0.17 to +0.15). Pairing costs nothing and can only help; it
did not help here, and the 22 pairs did the work.

**The edge of the search.** Seven choices at the largest rate tried is
what the Deep Learning Tuning Playbook warns of: "If the best points
cluster towards the edge of a search space (in some dimension), then the
search space boundaries might need to be expanded until the best observed
point is no longer close to the boundary." So one further small run, at
08.03's depth, four blocks, past the top:

```
$ python3 -m experiments.depth "depths=[4]" "rates=[0.012,0.024,0.048]" seeds=3 steps=300 every=100 out=experiments/recorded/followup
```

96 seconds. At 0.024 both models are worse than at 0.012 (3.415 against
3.379 without positions, 3.235 against 3.210 with them), and at 0.048
far worse (3.867 and 4.045): at four blocks 0.012 was the best rate, not
a limit of the range. Its three pairs ran at the experiment's own rates
and seeds, and gave its seeds 1 to 3 again, file for file, the same
bytes. The other depths' edges were not followed up.

## How it works

### Chosen on one text, reported on another

```
    return {key: min(scores, key=scores.get) for key, scores in found.items()}
```

`found` holds only validation bits: `search` never asks `trained` for the
test text, and the recorded search runs' summary.json files have no test
bits in them. The test text is read once a model's rate is fixed, by the
runs of the pairs. The validation text is The Taming of the Shrew and the
test text its end and The Tempest, a little harder (07.01): for these
models by 0.014 to 0.024 bits; each gap is printed on both, and if the two told different
stories the test text's sampling would be the suspect.

### Copied units, then new ones

```
            self.up.weight[:hidden] = mlp.up.weight
```

The control starts as the model without positions of the same seed
starts, weight for weight, plus the new units: what it adds is weights and
nothing else. Started from scratch instead, the control would differ from
its pair by a whole new draw of its MLPs, and its gap would carry that
noise too.

### The load falls on every model alike

```
    for seed in range(1, cfg.seeds + 1):
```

The loops go seed, then depth, then model: the two models of a pair run
one after the other, and every depth gets its turn at each seed. On a
laptop shared with other work, a slow half hour then slows every model
alike. Times are printed, never written into a run's files, so the
recorded files are the same bytes however loaded the machine was.

## Decide the boundary

What a model is worth, here, depends on the rate it was given. This
experiment reports each model at the best rate its search found: what
positions are worth when both sides do as well as they can in 300 steps.
08.03 reported both at one rate: what they are worth at that setting. Both
are true, and they differ by a factor of three at four blocks. A paper
that compares two models at one rate, chosen for one of them, can find any
gap from nothing to a lot, and most do not say which rate they would have
needed. Should a comparison of two architectures give each its own search,
at a cost in runs, or one shared setting that is cheap and easy to rerun?

## Go deeper

- [Deep Learning Tuning Playbook](https://github.com/google-research/tuning_playbook) by Godbole, Dahl, Gilmer, Shallue and Nado - "Identifying scientific, nuisance, and fixed hyperparameters", on why the learning rate is tuned for each number of layers, and "Identifying bad search space boundaries", the reason for this lesson's follow-up.

- [Language Modeling with Deep Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04226) by Irie et al., 2019 - table 9: at 12 to 42 layers, models without positions ahead of sinusoidal ones, trained for 5 epochs; a budget far past 300 steps.
