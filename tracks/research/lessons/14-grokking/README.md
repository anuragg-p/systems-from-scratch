# Module 14 - Reproduce a paper: grokking

This module takes one published result, Power et al.'s grokking (2022), and finds out on the track's own code whether it holds: a small transformer trained on half of the table of equations x / y mod 97 learns its training equations in under a thousand steps and, about a thousand times later, the rest. It reads the paper first, for every setting with where the text gives it and for ten claims written down as numbers with rules fixed before any run, beside the authors' code, which scores the loss on an end token too and keeps one split for every run; a model that only memorises scores below a guess on the held-out equations. Then the experiment as the text describes it, with module 08's GPT unchanged and the paper's AdamW: at the paper's 50% this GPT memorises and generalises together, so it runs at 35%, and a page's minute of 350 steps memorises the training equations while the held-out ones stay at chance, which is what a learner who stopped there would report. The recorded run of 5000 steps draws the figure: every seed memorises near step 400 at chance and generalises 5.0 to 5.6 times later, not the paper's thousand, and its held-out loss rises in between to 1.16 times a guess's, against figure 4's 9. The ablations show without weight decay no generalising at all, less data steeply slower, and, at the paper's own 50%, weights started 10 times larger bringing back a delay of 4 to 8 times: a detail the paper's text does not give decides the result. The report sets every claim beside ours, read from the logs: seven held, two held in direction only, one did not. It stands on module 03's AdamW and warmup, module 05's held-out data, and module 08's GPT. After it the track has reproduced a result end to end, and knows what a reproduction can and cannot settle.
