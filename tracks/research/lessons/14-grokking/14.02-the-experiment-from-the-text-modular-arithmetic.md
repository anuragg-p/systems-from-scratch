# 14.02 The experiment from the text: modular arithmetic

The paper's experiment, run as its text describes it: a transformer of 2
layers, width 128 and 4 heads, which is module 08's GPT at those sizes,
trained with AdamW at the paper's rate, betas and weight decay, warmed up
over 10 steps, on batches of 512, scored on its guess at the answer alone.
One setting differs from figure 1: 35% of the equations to train on, not
50%, because at 50% this GPT never memorises first. The page's run is 350
steps of the paper's 10^5. In it every seed memorises most of its
training equations while the held-out ones stay at chance. A learner who
stopped here would report a model that memorises and does not generalise.

## Where this fits

![Where 14.02 fits](figures/14.02-map.svg)

This lesson's piece is the reproduction again: its training loop,
`experiments/arithmetic.py`, which 14.03 and 14.04 share, and the
experiment that runs it, `experiments/grokking.py`. Below it are the GPT of
08.01 to 08.03, used exactly as module 08 built it, 03.04's AdamW, 03.05's
warmup and 03.01's way of cutting a training set into batches. After this
lesson the track can train the paper's model on the paper's data with the
paper's optimiser, and measure both halves of the table at steps spaced
on a log scale, as the paper's figure is drawn.

## The idea

### The model: module 08's GPT at the paper's sizes

The text asks for "a standard decoder-only transformer ... with causal
attention masking", 2 layers, width 128 and 4 heads, "about 4 x 10^5
non-embedding parameters". That is the GPT of 08.01 to 08.03 with those
settings and a vocabulary of 101 tokens, the residues and the four symbols
of 14.01: 410,240 weights, 396,800 of them outside the embeddings and
positions. It reads a, /, b and =, and its scores at the last of the four
are its guess at c. The causal mask (08.01) lets that position see all
four.

The authors' code builds its transformer differently in six ways the text
never mentions: layer norms after each addition instead of before, ReLU
instead of GELU, fixed sines and cosines for positions instead of learned
ones, an output layer of its own instead of the embedding table, no biases,
and PyTorch's own starting weights, far larger than the GPT-2 start of
08.02. Since the text names none of them, none is changed here. 14.04
measures what one of them does.

### The optimiser and the batches

AdamW (03.04) at a rate of 10^-3, with betas 0.9 and 0.98 and weight decay
1 on every weight: each step multiplies every weight by 1 - 0.001 x 1 =
0.999 before Adam's step. The rate rises over the first 10 updates, 03.05's
**warmup**, and then stays where it is: the paper "chose not to anneal the
learning rate". Batches are 512 equations, "or half of training dataset
size (whichever was smaller)", in a new random order every pass, from 03.01's
`batches()`.

### The loss on the answer alone

"calculated loss and accuracy only on the answer part of the equation":
the cross-entropy (02.04) of the scores at the last position against c, in
nats, and the fraction of equations whose highest score is c's. A guess
scores ln 97 = 4.575 and 1/97 = 0.0103.

### Steps on a log scale

The paper's figure 1 has a log axis from 10 to 10^6 steps: each factor of
ten gets the same width, so a thousand-fold delay is three equal steps
across the page. A run is measured the same way, at steps spaced evenly in
the logarithm: step 0, a few in each factor of ten, and the last. Each
measurement scores the whole training set and the whole held-out set.

### Why 35% and not 50%

At the paper's 50%, this GPT generalises as it memorises. In 14.04's
recorded runs at 50%, seeds 0 and 1 reach 99% on the training equations
and on the held-out ones at the same measured step or the next, 630 and
630, 630 and 794; seed 2 finds neither in 6000 steps. There is no
memorising first. The paper itself says where to look, section 3.1: "We
found these behaviors to be typical for all the binary operations for
dataset sizes that were close to the minimal dataset size for which the
network generalized within the allotted optimization budget. For larger
dataset sizes, the training and validation curves tend to track each other
more closely." So the fraction comes down, to 35%: one change from figure
1's set-up, and one the paper itself varies, in figure 1's centre panel.

![Accuracy on the training equations (dashed) and on the held-out ones (solid), 3 seeds, a dot where each first reached 99%: the page's run at 35% of the equations, and the recorded runs of 14.04 at the paper's 50%](figures/14.02-short.svg)

### `log_steps`

The steps at which a run is measured: 0, then `per_decade` steps in every
factor of ten, 10 to the power k / `per_decade` rounded down without
repeats, and the last step.

### `score`

Accuracy and loss of the guess at c on rows of equations, read at the last
of the first four tokens, in eval mode and without a graph.

### `train`

One seed of the paper's training: AdamW, the warmup, the batches, the loss
at c only, and at each of `log_steps()` both halves scored whole into the
run's log.

### What the run does

`experiments/grokking.py` builds the data and the GPT for each seed, calls
`train()`, and prints the end of the log as 00.03's spread over seeds,
with the curves of every seed drawn by the lab. The default is 3 seeds of
350 steps, measured 5 times a decade.

**What the page's run shows, and what it cannot.** By step 350 the three
seeds stand at 0.9844 and 0.9911 and 0.9165 on their training equations:
one has passed 99%, two have not quite. The recorded long run of 14.03,
the same set-up for 5000 steps, has its seeds pass 99% between steps 354
and 446, so 400 steps would not show it for every seed either. Stopped
here, the run says what a learner without the paper would report: the
model memorises and does not generalise, 05.01's overfitting. Its
budget is 350 of the paper's 10^5 steps.

What a step costs: the model reads 512 equations of 4 tokens, about 5
billion floating-point operations forward and backward, 31 to 35 ms on
this laptop's CPU with another training run going. A minute holds about
1500 steps; the paper's 10^5 is about an hour for one seed, and figure
1's 10^6 about ten hours.

## How it works

### Half the set when that is less

```
    size = min(cfg.batch, math.ceil(len(train_rows) / 2))
```

At 35% there are 3259 training equations, so the batch is the paper's
512, and a pass is 6 batches of 512 and a seventh of 187: the rest. The
text gives the batch size and says nothing of the rest. The authors' code
takes it as a smaller last batch, as `batches()` does. Break it, below,
shows what that rest can do.

### Warmed up, never lowered

```
    scheduler = torch.optim.lr_scheduler.LambdaLR(opt, lab.warmup_cosine(cfg.warmup, cfg.steps, floor=1.0))
```

03.05's `warmup_cosine()` multiplies the rate by (step + 1) / 10 for the
first 10 updates, then runs half a cosine from 1 down to its floor. With
the floor at 1 the cosine runs from 1 to 1: the rate stays at 10^-3 to the
end, as the paper's "chose not to anneal" asks. The authors' code ramps as
step / 10, so its first update is at rate 0; ours is a tenth. A one-step
difference, not measured.

### Measured whole, and only where the axis needs it

```
            if step in at:
                measure(step)
```

Scoring both halves whole, 9312 equations, costs about four steps of
training, so a run measures only at the steps of `log_steps()`: 14 of them
in 350 steps. The training accuracy is the whole training set's at that
moment, not the mean of the batches it trained on over a pass, which is
what the authors' code logs: a pass's mean lags the model and can never
show a dip that lasts less than a pass.

## Decide the boundary

The text's set-up at the paper's own 50% gives no figure with this GPT:
both halves rise together. The page moved one setting until the effect
appeared, and says so. The other choice is to run 50% as written and report
that grokking did not happen, which is true too, and which 14.04's runs
show. A reproduction that changes a setting tests a claim the paper did not
quite make; one that does not may test nothing. Which run should carry the
figure?

Nothing is learned. The training accuracy is 0.02557 after 350 steps,
where with 512 it was 0.964; the training loss, 4.517, has barely left a
guess's 4.575. 543 is not a bigger batch in any way that matters: 3259 = 6
x 543 + 1, so every pass now ends with a batch of one equation. Adam's
step is about the rate in size whatever the gradient's size (03.03), so
that one equation moves every weight about as far as a batch of 512 does,
once every seven steps. What exactly it undoes was not measured.

![One pass over the 3259 training equations, cut into batches of 512 (the paper's) and of 543; and the training accuracy each gives, 3 seeds](figures/14.02-batches.svg)

The paper's own batch size meets it too, at another fraction. At 33% of
the equations there are 3073, which is 6 x 512 + 1: in a trial run of
this script with `fraction=0.33`, the training accuracy was 0.02473 after
350 steps. At 33.2%, 3092 equations and a last batch of 20, it was 0.1171:
slower than at 35%, and learning. No paper reports the size of its last
batch, and here it decides whether the model learns at all. Put
`batch=512` back.
