# Module 12 - Fine-tuning

Module 09 left GPT-2's published weights in the learner's own GPT; this module changes what that model does without training it again from nothing, and measures each change before and after. A new Linear head on GPT-2's vector at the end of a question sorts Banking77's bank questions into 77 intents, Radford et al.'s recipe of 2018: on GPT-2's vectors as they come the head learns almost nothing, 0.187 of the test questions right, until each of their 768 numbers is standardised (0.773), and with the body frozen it stays behind a bag of words tuned as carefully. Fine-tuning every weight, in the module's recorded run, reaches 0.900 against 0.793 for the same kind of model trained from nothing: the paper's claim held. LoRA, Hu et al.'s, trains a change of low rank beside two weight matrices of each block, starts as GPT-2 exactly and merges back into a plain GPT: with under 0.3% of the weights it reaches 0.886 to 0.889, rank 1 as good as rank 8, for a third of the memory training adds; ahead of every weight early in training and 1.1 to 1.4 points behind it at the end, so "on par or better" did not hold at this budget. A minute of LoRA on instructions made by code, in a chat template of plain markers, takes GPT-2 from answering none of 180 questions to 0.375 of new instances right, 0.342 in a wording it never saw, while it answers the one task it was never shown with the wrong number. Last, what each fine-tune cost on text it was not tuned on: LoRA forgot less than every weight on held-out TinyStories and more on Shakespeare, though it moved the weights further, so the distance moved does not predict forgetting. It stands on module 03's AdamW and schedule, module 08's GPT and its greedy writing, and module 09's tokenizer and GPT-2. After it the learner can make someone else's weights do their task, on a laptop, and say what that bought and what it cost.
