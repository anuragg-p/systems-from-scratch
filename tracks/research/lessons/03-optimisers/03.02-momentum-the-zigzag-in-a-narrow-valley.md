# 03.02 Momentum: the zigzag in a narrow valley

In 03.01 the learning rate had a ceiling: past it, steps land further from
the bottom than they started. The ceiling is set by the steepest direction,
and in a loss that is steep one way and shallow another, a rate under it
crawls along the shallow way. This lesson gives SGD a memory of its past
steps, momentum, so that pushes that agree add up and pushes that flip
cancel.

## Where this fits

![Where 03.02 fits](figures/03.02-map.svg)

This lesson is the second box of the module's strip, in the engine's
optimisers, which 03.01 started with plain SGD. The optimiser keeps the
same place: under every training loop, above the tensors and layers it
moves. What changes is what it remembers: from here on SGD can keep a
velocity for each parameter from one step to the next. After this lesson
the engine's SGD takes PyTorch's `momentum`, `dampening` and `nesterov`
settings, and a network on MNIST gets further in one pass at the same
rate.

## The idea

![Plain gradient descent and momentum 0.8 in the valley (w0^2 + 50 w1^2) / 2 from (-4, 1), every step a dot: plain descent bounces across and then creeps along the floor for 186 steps; momentum swings across but runs along the floor and is at the bottom in 52](figures/03.02-valley.svg)

Take a valley with two weights, w0 along its floor and w1 across it, and
the loss (w0^2 + 50 w1^2) / 2: its walls curve 50 times more steeply than
its floor. By 03.01's algebra the rate must stay below 2 / 50 = 0.04, or
w1 is thrown further up the walls with every step. At lr 0.03 each step
multiplies w1 by 1 - 0.03 x 50 = -0.5, so it bounces from wall to wall,
halving each time, and multiplies w0 by 1 - 0.03 = 0.97: it creeps along
the floor, 3% a step. The figure's upper path is that run: across in a
few steps, then 186 steps of creeping before the loss is below 0.0001.

**Momentum** keeps a **velocity** for each weight: each step, the old
velocity times a factor mu (the momentum, 0.8 here) plus the new gradient,
and the weight moves by lr times the velocity, not the gradient. Along the
floor the gradient points the same way step after step, so the velocity
keeps adding to itself and the steps grow, towards five times the
gradient's at mu 0.8. Across the valley the gradient flips sign every step,
so each new gradient cancels most of the velocity it is added to. The lower
path is the same valley, rate and start with mu 0.8: it still swings across
the valley, but it runs along it and is at the bottom in 52 steps.

### `momentum`

`SGD` gains PyTorch's three settings, `momentum`, `dampening` and
`nesterov`, all off by default so that plain SGD is unchanged; its `step()`
now asks this method for the direction whenever the group's momentum is not
0. The velocity of each parameter lives in `self.state[p]` as
`momentum_buffer`. On the first step it is the gradient itself; after that
it is momentum times the old buffer plus (1 - dampening) times the
gradient. Nesterov's variant, which 03.03 uses, moves by the gradient plus
momentum times the new buffer: a look at where the velocity is about to
carry the weight.

### `valley`

The valley as a two-weight tensor, trained by the engine's own SGD: from
the start, step until the loss is below the threshold or the steps run out,
keeping every point of the path. The script runs it once without momentum
and once with, then trains 03.01's network on MNIST for one pass three
ways: plain SGD at 0.05, momentum 0.9 at the same 0.05, and plain SGD at
0.5, 03.01's best rate.

## How it works

### Ten times the push, half the bounce

Feed the velocity the same gradient g every step. After n steps it is
`g (1 + mu + mu^2 + ... + mu^(n-1)) = g (1 - mu^n) / (1 - mu)`, which grows towards g / (1 - mu): 5 g at mu 0.8, 10 g at 0.9. Feed it a
gradient that flips sign every step instead, g, -g, g, ..., and the same
sum has alternating signs, (1 - (-mu)^n) / (1 + mu), which settles at
g / (1 + mu): about half of g at 0.9. Along the floor the step is ten times
plain descent's; across it, half. A check in numbers, 50 steps of the
engine's SGD at mu 0.9 on a weight whose gradient is always 1, and on one
whose gradient flips, in the project's folder:

<!-- verify: session -->
```
$ cd build/at/research/03.02
$ python3 -c "
import engine as torch
for flip in (1, -1):
    w = torch.tensor([0.0], requires_grad=True)
    opt = torch.optim.SGD([w], lr=1.0, momentum=0.9)
    for step in range(50):
        opt.zero_grad()
        (w * flip ** step).sum().backward()
        opt.step()
    print(flip, opt.state[w]['momentum_buffer'].round(4))
"
1 [9.9485]
-1 [-0.5236]
$ cd ../../../..
```

(1 - 0.9^50) / 0.1 = 9.9485, and -(1 - 0.9^50) / 1.9 = -0.5236: the
velocity is the sum above, to four decimals.

The same sums say why mu 0.95 was slow in the valley. With a velocity that
forgets only 5% a step, the weight keeps going long after the gradient has
turned against it, and swings past the bottom again and again. Momentum
trades a faster run along the floor for swinging; 0.8 was the better trade
here; the MNIST run used 0.9.

### The state is per parameter

`self.state` is a dictionary keyed by the parameter itself, so each weight
tensor has its own velocity, of its own shape, and an optimiser over a
whole network keeps one buffer per parameter. That is the memory the method
adds, and the only thing it adds: the buffer is updated in place
(`buf *= ...`, `buf += ...`), so it is never copied after the first step.

## Decide the boundary

This SGD steps by lr times the velocity, as PyTorch does. Sutskever and his
co-authors write it the other way round: the velocity is momentum times the
old one minus lr times the gradient, and the weight moves by the velocity.
With a fixed rate the two take the same steps. When a schedule changes the
rate (03.05), PyTorch's form applies the new rate to the whole velocity at
once, the other only to new gradients. Which should a rate change affect:
every past push, or only the next?

## Break it

Take the friction away. In `engine/optim/__init__.py`, in `momentum()`,
delete the line that shrinks the old velocity:

<!-- verify: patch file=engine/optim/__init__.py
-            buf *= group["momentum"]
-->

```
            buf *= group["momentum"]    <- delete this line
```

## Go deeper

- [Why Momentum Really Works](https://distill.pub/2017/momentum/) by Gabriel Goh, Distill 2017 - "The Dynamics of Momentum" and "Optimal parameters": momentum on a quadratic, direction by direction, as a damped oscillator; why the best momentum and rate square-root the condition number (the ratio of the steepest curve to the shallowest, 50 here); and how momentum widens the range of rates that do not diverge. The playgrounds let you set the step and the momentum and watch the path.
- [On the importance of initialization and momentum in deep learning](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/sutskever13.html) by Sutskever, Martens, Dahl and Hinton, ICML 2013 - section 2: classical momentum as equations (1) and (2), Nesterov's accelerated gradient as (3) and (4), and figure 1, the picture of the difference: Nesterov's takes the gradient after the velocity's move, which corrects a bad velocity sooner.
