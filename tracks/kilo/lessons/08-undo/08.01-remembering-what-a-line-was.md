# 08.01 Remembering what a line was

Before a row changes, kilo now keeps a copy of what it was, and Ctrl-Z puts
it back. This lesson makes those copies and takes them back, one key at a
time; redo comes in the next.

## Where this fits

![Where 08.01 fits](figures/08.01-map.svg)

This lesson builds two pieces of the map: the records, just under the rows
at the right, and the history, the long box under everything. Just before
a row changes, the records piece is told which row and how, and keeps what
the row was in the history. Ctrl-Z, a new case of the key handler, takes
the newest step out of the history and puts its rows back, the arrow up
the right side, and the frame draws them as after any edit. After this
lesson a slip of the finger is no longer permanent: Ctrl-Z takes it back,
a key at a time, as far back as the file was opened.

## The idea

So far an edit in kilo cannot be taken back. Delete a line you meant to
keep, and the only way to get it back is to quit without saving, which
throws away every other change since the last save too.

The idea is to remember before you forget: just before a row changes, keep
what it was. A **record** says what is about to happen to one row, and so
what to put back. There are three kinds:

- `U_CHG`: the row is about to change. The record keeps a copy of its text.
- `U_INS`: a row is about to be inserted. The record keeps nothing: the row
  was not there.
- `U_DEL`: the row is about to be deleted. The record keeps its text.

Putting a record back does the opposite of the change: a `U_CHG` gives the
row its old text, a `U_INS` deletes the row, a `U_DEL` inserts it again.

A **step** is what one Ctrl-Z takes back: the records it holds, in the order
they were made. In this lesson every key is a step. Most keys that edit make
one record, but not all:

![Three keys and the records each makes. A character typed: one U_CHG, the row as it was. Enter in the middle of a row: a U_INS of the new row below, then a U_CHG of the row it is cut from. Backspace at the start of a row: a U_CHG of the row above, which the row is joined onto, then a U_DEL of the row, with its text](figures/08.01-records.svg)

Ctrl-Z takes the newest step and puts its records back **last to first**.
Take Enter pressed after `and ` in the row `and what each line was`. First
a new row is inserted below, holding `what each line was`: a `U_INS`. Then
the row is cut after `and `: a `U_CHG`, keeping `and what each line was`.
Ctrl-Z applies the `U_CHG` first, and the row is whole again; then the
`U_INS`, and the row below goes.

![Ctrl-Z after Enter in the middle of a row, step by step: the rows before Enter; after it, with the step's two records, U_INS then U_CHG; Ctrl-Z applies the U_CHG first, and the cut row is whole again; then the U_INS, and the row Enter made is deleted](figures/08.01-undo-enter.svg)

This is Vim's design, cut to kilo's size. Before a change to its lines,
Vim calls `u_save()`, which keeps the lines between two line numbers, top
and bot, and an undo puts the kept lines back. Vim's entry holds a range of
lines; kilo's record holds one row, and a step as many records as it
needs.

<!-- verify: mention names="u_save()" outside reason="Vim's function, not this project's" -->

### `undoSave`

Called by the row functions just before they change a row, with the kind of
change and the row's number. It fills a record, copying the row's text and
its `'\0'` for a `U_CHG` or a `U_DEL`, opens a new step on the **undo
stack** if none is open, and adds the record to that step.

### `undoClose`

Ends the open step, so that the next record starts a new one. For now
`editorProcessKeypress()` calls it for every key, before the key acts: that
is what makes every key a step.

### `undoPut`

Puts a row back as a record says it was: the record's text becomes the
row's for a `U_CHG`, the row is deleted for a `U_INS`, and inserted with
the record's text for a `U_DEL`. The text is used up.

### `editorUndo`

Ctrl-Z. It closes the open step; with no step on the undo stack it says
`Nothing to undo`. Otherwise it takes the newest step off the stack, puts
its records back last to first, and frees it: nothing does a step again yet.

Outside these: the three kinds; the structs for a record, a step and the
history, and `U`, the history itself; `undoFreeStep()`, `undoClear()`, `undoGiveUp()` and
`undoPush()`; the calls of `undoSave()` in the row functions; `undoClear()`
at the top of `editorOpen()`; and the keys Ctrl-Z and Ctrl-R, whose case
does nothing yet.

## How it works

### Records are made low down

![The three key functions on top, editorInsertChar, editorInsertNewline and editorDelChar, and the five row functions under them, a line for each call. A record is made in each of the five row functions, and at the one place editorInsertNewline cuts a row by hand](figures/08.01-hooks.svg)

The records could be made in each key's own function, each knowing which
rows its key touches. They are made lower down. Every change to the text
goes through five row functions, `editorInsertRow()`, `editorDelRow()`,
`editorRowInsertChar()`, `editorRowAppendString()` and `editorRowDelChar()`,
or through the one place where `editorInsertNewline()` cuts a row by hand.
Each calls `undoSave()` first, as `editorInsertRow()` does:

```
    undoSave(U_INS,at);
```

and the cut:

```
        undoSave(U_CHG,filerow);    /* the row is cut here, by hand */
```

So whatever edits through them is remembered with no code of its own. Enter
makes two records and a join two, and neither key knows.

### Last to first

Each record was made against the text as it stood then, after the changes
before it in its step. So an undo walks back through those moments in the
other order: the last record first, against the text as the step left it,
then the one before it, and so on.

```
    for (j = s.numrec-1; j >= 0; j--)
        undoPut(&s.rec[j]);
```

This loop keeps the **invariant**: a record's row is inside the file when
the record is applied (or one past the last row, for a `U_DEL`, which
inserts there). Break it shows what happens when it is not.

### Nothing is remembered while undo works

`undoPut()` changes rows through the same row functions, so each of its
changes would call `undoSave()` and make a record of the undo itself.
`editorUndo()` sets `U.off` around its loop, and `undoSave()` starts:

```
    if (U.off || U.lost) return;
```

Reading a file is not an edit either. `editorOpen()` inserts every line it
reads with `editorInsertRow()`; with recording on, the whole file would be
one step, and Ctrl-Z in a file just opened would delete every row. So:

```
    U.off = 1;                      /* reading the file is not an edit */
```

just before its loop, and `U.off = 0` just after, not at the top and the
end of `editorOpen()`: for a file that does not exist it returns early, and
recording would stay off for the whole session.

### When there is no memory for a record

`undoSave()` cannot stop the change it is told about. If `malloc()` fails,
it gives the history up instead:

```
nomem:
    free(r.text);
    undoGiveUp("out of memory");
```

`undoGiveUp()` frees every step, says `undo: out of memory`, and sets
`U.lost` until the step ends, so that the rest of the step is not kept
either: half a step undone would put back a text that never was. The
change itself is made.

## Decide the boundary

kilo's record holds a whole row, as Vim's holds whole lines (its entry's
`ue_array` is the "array of lines in undo block"). That fits a text kept as
lines, and giving a row its old text back is the assignment of a pointer;
the cost is that a character typed into a long row keeps a copy of the
whole row. Emacs keeps positions and characters instead: an insertion is
`(BEG . END)`, with no text, a deletion `(TEXT . POSITION)`, the text taken
out; VS Code keeps an old and a new text with their positions. A record is
then as small as the change, however long the line. Helix keeps each
transaction with its inversion, "because delete transactions don't store
the deleted text". Zed makes an undo an operation of its own, which counts
the edits it undoes, each named by a Lamport timestamp: an edit is undone
while its count is odd. Simplest of all would be a copy of the whole text
at every step: nothing to get wrong, and a copy of the file for every key.
What is your text made of, and who else is changing it?

## Break it

Put the records back first to last. In `build/at/kilo/08.01/kilo.c`, in
`editorUndo()`, change the loop:

<!-- verify: patch file=kilo.c
-    for (j = s.numrec-1; j >= 0; j--)
+    for (j = 0; j < s.numrec; j++)
-->
<!-- verify: skip reason="the line as the reader changes it; the patch above makes the change" -->
```
    for (j = 0; j < s.numrec; j++)
```

```
./learn run kilo --at 08.01 -- notes.txt
```

Do Run it again, the eight keys and eight Ctrl-Z: every screen is the same.
Enter's two records are of two rows, the cut row and the new one below it,
and neither moves the other, so either order puts them back; the join's
two are the same. Now press Down twice, onto the row under the last, and
type `X`. kilo first adds a row there for it, then puts the `X` in it: a
`U_INS` and a `U_CHG` of the same row. Press Ctrl-Z.

kilo is gone at once. Its screen stays, `X` and all, the shell's prompt
comes back over it, and the terminal is left in raw mode: type `reset` and
Enter to set it right. The `U_INS`, applied first, deleted the row; the
`U_CHG` then put its text into a row no longer in the file, and on the way
`undoPut()` freed that row's text a second time. On this Mac the system's `malloc()`
caught it ("not an allocated block", says the crash report) and stopped
kilo with signal 5, SIGTRAP. Built with AddressSanitizer (`cc
-fsanitize=address`), kilo says `attempting double-free` in `undoPut()`,
of 2 bytes `editorFreeRow()` freed a moment before. Put the line back.
